We are glad to perceive that some attempt it now making to remedy the great want experienced in the colony, of a proper system of appeals against the almost irresponsible decisions of magistrates engaged in dispensing justice amongst us. A tendency has shown itself of late years in England, and one which we think should be most zealously guarded against—to leave a great deal of criminal and quasi-criminal justice to be dealt with by a magistrate in a summary manner. We cannot help regarding every movement of this kind as a departure from the venerable English principle that every man should be tried by a jury of his peers. There is too much of a tendency to imperialism— to autocracy—in it. But since a great deal of our law is to be administered in that way, it should be our duty to see that the evils to be apprehended from an abuse of magisterial power should be obviated as much as possible by giving the accused a further hearing and before another Court. We all know how long the colony has been without a sufficient number of magistrates to constitute a Court of Quarter Sessions. Up to the time of the election of the Mayor, and for a long time previous, the Magisterial power in this community could be exercised only by our Police Commissioner, from whose decision, as a general thing, there was not a single court to appeal to.

Since the election of the Mayor, whatever may be the cause, we are no better. The necessity then of some such rights as those given by Mr. Ring in his Bill lately introduced into the House of Assembly is quite apparent. It is proposed to give a right to appeal from the decision of a stipendiary or other magistrate in all cases summarily adjudged upon. The appeal is to be to the Supreme Court, but is not to do away with the ordinary right of appeal to the Court of Quarter Sessions when such a court exists. The appealed cause may be heard eight days after notice of appeal shall have been given to the Supreme Court. The notice itself must contain a synopsis of the grounds of appeal. The costs, however, must be guaranteed before anything can be done. This will have the tendency to prevent frivolous and groundless applications for reversal of the decision of the inferior magistrate. The bill also provides that an accused, whenever charged before a magistrate with a cause, may demand to be tried by a jury and not by the magistrate, and shall have the right to have his case sent to the Quarter Sessions or Supreme Court for trial, and in the meantime to be admitted to bail in bailable cases. On the whole we are inclined to believe the change sought to be effected in the present unsatisfactory and irresponsible system of administering justice will be found to be of the utmost benefit to the inhabitants of the Colony, as it will go far under to away with any evil effect of wrong magisterial decisions, whether they be caused inadvertently, or by wilfulness, ignorance, or incompetence.

The U.S. Mail steamer Eliza Anderson arrived at 8 o'clock, bringing the following dispatches. The Anderson brought a large freight of cattle, sheep and horses.

Pleasant Valley, Oct. 17th — The rebel Gen. Stuart is at a point four miles from Leetown on the Occoquan river. Colonel Lee is at Stephenson depot, five miles east of Winchester. Gen. Longstreet is ten miles above Winchester. Stonewall Jackson is at Bunker Hill.

New York, Oct. 17 — Draft in this State ordered for Nov. 10, the number to be probably thirty-five thousand.

Cairo, Oct. 17th.—A Federal force from Shawneetown started on Wednesday in pursuit of the rebels who robbed the steamer Hazel Dell. The mails taken from her have already been recovered.

Price is gathering a large number of troops at Holly Springs, Miss., with a view, it is said, of attacking some point on the Mobile and Ohio railroad.
